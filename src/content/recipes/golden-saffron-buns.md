ingredients:
  - "0.5g high-grade saffron threads"
  - "1 tbsp granulated sugar (for grinding saffron)"
  - "250ml whole milk, warm (approx. 38°C / 100°F)"
  - "25g fresh yeast (or 7g active dry yeast)"
  - "80g granulated sugar"
  - "100g unsalted butter, softened at room temperature"
  - "1 large egg, room temperature"
  - "1/2 tsp fine sea salt"
  - "450g bread flour or strong all-purpose flour"
  - "1/3 cup dark raisins (soaked in warm water for 15 minutes)"
  - "1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp milk (for egg wash)"
  - "2 tbsp Swedish pearl sugar (optional garnish)"
instructions:
  - "In a mortar, grind the saffron threads with 1 tbsp sugar until a fine crimson powder forms. Stir into the warm milk and let steep for 10 minutes until deeply golden."
  - "In a large mixing bowl or stand mixer, dissolve the yeast in the saffron-infused milk. Add the remaining sugar, softened butter, egg, and salt."
  - "Gradually incorporate the flour. Knead using a dough hook on medium speed for 8-10 minutes until the dough is smooth, elastic, and pulls cleanly away from the sides."
  - "Cover the bowl with a damp cloth and let rise in a warm spot for 60-75 minutes until doubled in volume."
  - "Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Divide into 12 equal portions. Roll each piece into a rope (approx. 25 cm) and shape into classic S-scrolls (Lussekatter) by curling the ends in opposite directions."
  - "Place buns on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Press a soaked raisin into the center of each spiral. Cover and proof for 30 minutes."
  - "Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Brush buns gently with egg wash and sprinkle with pearl sugar if desired."
  - "Bake for 10-12 minutes until puffy, golden, and fragrant. Transfer immediately to a wire rack and brush with a touch of melted butter for a glossy finish."
---
Saffron is chemically one of the most concentrated carotenoid compounds in modern botany, capable of turning mundane yeast dough into radiant winter gold. Our sensory matrix logs an immediate uplift in perceived holiday coziness whenever crocin molecules are activated by warm milk and butter. Pairing with sweet rehydrated raisins provides targeted bursts of natural acidity against the rich, buttery crumb.
