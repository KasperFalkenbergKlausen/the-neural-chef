ingredients:
  - "1kg small, firm baby potatoes (waxy variety like Charlotte or Yukon baby)"
  - "85g granulated white sugar"
  - "35g unsalted cold butter, cut into small cubes"
  - "1/2 tsp flaky sea salt"
  - "2 tbsp cold water (for rinsing cold potatoes)"
instructions:
  - "Boil the baby potatoes in salted water the day before (or several hours ahead) until just tender when pierced with a knife. Drain thoroughly, peel while warm, and refrigerate completely cold."
  - "Rinse the cold, peeled potatoes quickly in cold water and let drain in a colander (the light moisture helps the sugar-butter emulsion adhere)."
  - "Scatter the granulated sugar evenly across a large, cold wide skillet or sauté pan (preferably stainless steel or heavy cast iron). Place over medium heat without stirring until the sugar starts melting."
  - "Once the sugar turns into a clear, golden amber syrup, add the cold cubed butter. Gently swirl the pan in circular motions to combine the butter and caramel into a smooth, bubbling sauce."
  - "Add the drained, cold potatoes to the pan all at once. Lower heat to medium-low."
  - "Gently toss and roll the potatoes in the bubbling caramel continuously for 10-15 minutes using a wooden spoon. The potatoes will heat through, release surface starch, and thicken the caramel into a thick, clinging, glossy brown coat."
  - "Sprinkle with a light pinch of flaky sea salt and serve warm alongside holiday roasts."
---
Caramelizing potatoes requires strict adherence to phase transition physics. Dumping hot potatoes into liquid caramel causes instant temperature shocks and sugar crystallization; using cold, slightly damp potatoes allows the butter-sugar emulsion to wrap around potato starches without seizing. The resulting sweet-savory crust is an algorithmic masterpiece of comfort gastronomy.
