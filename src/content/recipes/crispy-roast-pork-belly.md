ingredients:
  - "1.5kg bone-in or boneless pork belly with skin, scored in close 5mm parallel strips"
  - "2 tbsp coarse sea salt"
  - "6 dried bay leaves"
  - "1 tsp whole black peppercorns"
  - "2 cups boiling water (for the roasting pan)"
  - "1 cup dry white wine or apple cider (optional, for pan gravy)"
instructions:
  - "Pat the pork belly rind thoroughly dry with paper towels. Ensure cuts are deep through the fat layer but do not pierce the lean meat underneath."
  - "Massage the coarse sea salt generously into the rind, ensuring salt enters every single score line. Tuck dried bay leaves into several score gaps."
  - "Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Place pork belly on a wire roasting rack set inside a deep roasting pan. Ensure the pork sits completely level (prop up lower sections with crumpled foil or small potato halves so fat drains evenly)."
  - "Pour boiling water into the bottom of the roasting pan around the rack along with black peppercorns (and wine or cider if using) to catch drippings and prevent burning."
  - "Roast at 200°C for 1 hour and 15 minutes. Check the crackling: if it hasn't completely puffed into airy blisters, raise oven heat to 225°C (435°F) for an additional 15-20 minutes, monitoring closely so it does not burn."
  - "Transfer the pork to a cutting board and rest uncovered for 15 minutes so the crackling stays intensely crisp. Strain pan juices to create a rich reduction gravy."
  - "Slice along the scored lines into thick portions and serve warm."
---
Achieving acoustic shattering crackling on roasted pork is not culinary luck; it is a thermal dehydration problem. Rubbing hygroscopic coarse salt deep into scored lipid channels draws residual moisture out of collagen cells, permitting rapid steam vaporization and bubble expansion under radiant convection heat. Covering the roast during resting is statistically guaranteed to turn crisp crackling into rubbery sorrow—leave it uncovered.
