ingredients:
  - "1 whole duck (approx. 2.8 - 3.2 kg), thawed and innards removed"
  - "3 tart cooking apples (such as Cox Orange or Bramley), cored and cut into thick wedges"
  - "150g dried pitted prunes"
  - "2 tbsp coarse sea salt"
  - "1 tsp freshly ground black pepper"
  - "1 tsp dried thyme"
  - "3 cups cold water (for roasting pan)"
  - "1 cup tawny port or dry red wine (for pan gravy)"
instructions:
  - "Trim excess skin and fat pads from the cavity openings and neck of the duck. Prick the fatty skin lightly all over with a skewer or fork, being careful not to pierce the meat below."
  - "Season the cavity generously with salt, pepper, and dried thyme. Tightly stuff the interior with the apple wedges and prunes. Tie legs together with kitchen twine and tuck wing tips back."
  - "Rub coarse sea salt thoroughly over the duck's entire skin."
  - "Preheat oven to 160°C (325°F). Place the duck breast-side down on a wire rack set in a deep roasting pan. Pour 3 cups water into the bottom of the pan to collect dripping fat."
  - "Roast for 1.5 hours. Carefully turn the duck breast-side up using tongs, increase oven temperature to 180°C (350°F), and roast for another 1 hour until tender and fat has rendered."
  - "Increase oven temperature to 220°C (425°F) for the final 15-20 minutes until the breast skin is blistered, dark golden, and audibly crisp. Watch closely to avoid charring."
  - "Transfer duck to a carving board and rest uncovered for 20 minutes before carving. Skim duck fat from roasting pan juices, deglaze with port wine, and simmer to create a concentrated holiday gravy."
---
Duck possesses an impressive subterranean lipid layer designed to insulate waterfowl against freezing ponds. In a culinary setting, this subterranean fat must be rendered out slowly over extended thermal durations before extreme radiant heat can polymerize the skin into an acoustic crisp. The stuffing of apples and prunes acts as an organic steam capsule, infusing fruit esters into the dark breast meat from the inside out.
