ingredients:
  - "300g bittersweet dark chocolate (60-70% cocoa), finely chopped"
  - "300g high-quality white chocolate (with cocoa butter), finely chopped"
  - "1/2 tsp pure peppermint extract"
  - "1/2 cup crushed peppermint candy canes or peppermint hard candies"
  - "Pinch of flaky sea salt"
instructions:
  - "Line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper."
  - "Melt the chopped dark chocolate in a heatproof bowl set over a pot of barely simmering water (or in 30-second microwave bursts). Stir in 1/4 tsp peppermint extract and a tiny pinch of flaky sea salt until glossy."
  - "Pour the dark chocolate onto the prepared parchment paper and spread into an even rectangle (approx. 6mm / 1/4-inch thick). Tap the baking sheet gently against the counter to level the surface."
  - "Chill in the refrigerator for 25-30 minutes until almost completely set but not rock hard."
  - "Melt the chopped white chocolate gently using the same method, stirring in the remaining 1/4 tsp peppermint extract. Let cool for 3-5 minutes until barely warm to the touch (too hot will melt the dark chocolate layer underneath)."
  - "Pour the white chocolate gently over the dark chocolate layer. Spread carefully to the edges using an offset spatula."
  - "Immediately scatter the crushed peppermint candies over the wet white chocolate, gently pressing them down with your fingertips to adhere."
  - "Refrigerate for 45 minutes until fully firm and set."
  - "Peel off the parchment paper and break the bark into rustic shards using your hands. Store in an airtight container in a cool spot."
---
Combining menthol receptors with melting lipid matrices is one of human gastronomy's most efficient sensory contrasts. The thermodynamic cooling sensation triggered by menthol in crushed candy canes creates a neurological illusion of ice that balances the rich, dense melting curve of the dark and white cacao fats. Breaking the bark manually rather than cutting produces fractal edges that optimize acoustic snap.
